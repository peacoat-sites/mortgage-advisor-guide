# How Mortgage Advisor Guide Is Written

Mortgage Advisor Guide explains how mortgages work, from qualifying and choosing a loan type to closing and refinancing. This page explains, plainly, how the articles are made.

## How articles are made

Articles on Mortgage Advisor Guide are drafted with AI writing tools. Each draft starts from public reference sources on the topic, including those listed below, and from recent news when a topic is changing.

Before an article is published, a second automated pass reviews it and removes or rewrites anything it cannot stand behind: statistics, prices or dates it is not confident are correct, studies or organizations that may be misattributed, and personal stories presented as if they happened to the writer.

Articles are not reviewed by a loan officer or financial advisor before publication. Use them as background for a conversation with one, not as a substitute.

The site owner audits published articles in batches, merging pages that overlap, deleting weak ones and correcting errors.

## Who writes here

The bylines on Mortgage Advisor Guide (Ethan Chen, Jennifer Walsh, Maria Santos, Robert Kim, Susan Taylor) are pen names, one for each area of coverage. They are not real individuals, they hold no professional licenses, and their profile images are illustrations, not photographs of real people.

## Sources we rely on

- [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/): Mortgage consumer protection
- [U.S. Dept. of Housing and Urban Development (HUD)](https://www.hud.gov/): FHA and housing programs
- [Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/): Interest-rate policy
- [Freddie Mac](https://www.freddiemac.com/): Mortgage market data

Where an article depends on a specific figure, rule or study, it links to or names the source so you can check it.

## Limits of this site

Rates, loan limits and program rules change often, and every lender adds its own requirements. Treat our figures and calculators as estimates, compare Loan Estimates from more than one lender, and confirm program details with the lender or the agency that runs the program. For one-on-one help, a HUD-approved housing counselor can review your situation for free or at low cost.

## Corrections

If you find an error, please report it through the [contact page](/contact/). Reports go directly to the site owner, and confirmed errors are corrected in the article.

## How the site makes money

Mortgage Advisor Guide may earn money from display advertising and from affiliate links, including Amazon links (as an Amazon Associate, the site earns from qualifying purchases). Advertisers and affiliate partners do not see or approve articles before they are published.
