The 2026 conforming loan limit for a one-unit home is **$832,750** in most of the United States. In high-cost counties it goes as high as **$1,249,125**. Those are the largest loan amounts Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac can buy this year, and anything above your county's limit is a [jumbo loan](/jumbo-loan-requirements/). The Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA) announced the figures on November 25, 2025, and they apply to loans the two companies acquire during calendar year 2026.

The 2027 limits have not been announced yet. FHFA normally publishes them in the last week of November, so the section on 2027 below explains what to expect and how some lenders are already moving.

## 2026 conforming loan limits by property type

| Property type | Baseline (most counties) | High-cost ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| 1 unit | $832,750 | $1,249,125 |
| 2 units | $1,066,250 | $1,599,375 |
| 3 units | $1,288,800 | $1,933,200 |
| 4 units | $1,601,750 | $2,402,625 |

The ceiling is 150% of the baseline in each column. Alaska, Hawaii, Guam and the U.S. Virgin Islands have their own statutory baseline, 50% above the national one: $1,249,125 for a one-unit home in 2026, with a one-unit ceiling of $1,873,675. The highest limit actually set in those areas for 2026 is $1,299,500, in Maui and Kalawao counties in Hawaii.

## How the limits changed from 2022 to 2026

| Year | Baseline (1 unit) | Change from prior year | High-cost ceiling (1 unit) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | $647,200 | | $970,800 |
| 2023 | $726,200 | +12.2% | $1,089,300 |
| 2024 | $766,550 | +5.6% | $1,149,825 |
| 2025 | $806,500 | +5.2% | $1,209,750 |
| 2026 | $832,750 | +3.3% | $1,249,125 |

Since 2022 the baseline has risen $185,550, or about 29%. Each yearly increase has been smaller than the one before because home price growth has slowed. By law the baseline moves by the same percentage as FHFA's House Price Index between the third quarter of one year and the third quarter of the next. For 2026 that change was 3.26%.

## What to expect for 2027

FHFA hasn't published the 2027 limits as of late November 2026. Last year's announcement came on November 25, the same day as the third-quarter House Price Index.

FHFA's monthly index showed prices up 2.4% from a year earlier in August 2026, [according to The Mortgage Reports](https://themortgagereports.com/137435/2027-conforming-loan-limits-preview). If the third-quarter figure came in at that rate, the 2027 baseline would be about $852,700 ($832,750 x 1.024, rounded down to the nearest $50 the way FHFA rounds). That is our arithmetic, not a forecast. The official number depends on the third-quarter data.

Some lenders aren't waiting. As of mid-November 2026, more than 50 lenders were making conforming loans above $832,750 using unofficial 2027 limits of $845,000, $847,440 or $850,000, as reported by National Mortgage Professional and The Mortgage Reports. These programs vary by lender and can come with conditions, such as a full appraisal. If your loan amount lands just above $832,750, it's worth asking whether a lender offers one before you accept jumbo terms.

## High-cost counties: who gets a higher limit

A county gets a limit above the baseline when 115% of its local median home value is higher than the baseline. The limit is set at that 115% figure, capped at the ceiling. FHFA uses median home values estimated by the Federal Housing Administration and applies the highest county median in a metro area to every county in that metro. FHFA also doesn't let a county's limit fall from one year to the next.

Our tabulation of FHFA's 2026 county file shows:

- **123 counties and county-equivalents** have a limit above the baseline, spread across 18 states and the District of Columbia.
- **38 are at the $1,249,125 ceiling.** They include Los Angeles, Orange, San Francisco, San Mateo, Santa Clara, Alameda and Contra Costa counties in California, Washington, D.C., most of Northern Virginia, Montgomery and Prince George's counties in Maryland, and resort counties such as Eagle (Colorado), Teton (Wyoming) and Nantucket (Massachusetts).
- **27 counties held at $1,209,750,** the 2025 ceiling. They include all five New York City boroughs, Nassau, Suffolk, Westchester and Rockland in New York, and 12 New Jersey counties including Bergen, Essex, Hudson and Morris. The formula didn't support a higher limit for these areas, so the no-decline policy kept them at last year's figure.
- **Other notable limits:** the Seattle area (King, Pierce and Snohomish) is $1,063,750, the Nashville area (Davidson, Williamson, Rutherford and nearby counties) is $1,029,250, and the Boston area (Suffolk, Middlesex, Norfolk, Essex and Plymouth) is $962,550.

## Conforming, high-balance or jumbo: where your loan lands

Your loan falls into one of three buckets, depending on its size and your county's limit:

1. **Standard conforming:** the loan is at or below the baseline ($832,750 for one unit). This applies everywhere.
2. **High-balance conforming:** the loan is above the baseline but at or below your county's higher limit. This only exists in high-cost counties. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac still buy these loans, though pricing can differ from a standard conforming loan. See our guide to [high-balance conforming loans](/high-balance-conforming-loan/).
3. **Jumbo:** the loan is above your county's limit. Lenders keep these loans or sell them to private investors, so each lender sets its own credit, reserve and down payment rules. See [jumbo loan requirements](/jumbo-loan-requirements/).

## The limit applies to the loan, not the price

What matters is the amount you borrow. Here's how the same house can land in different buckets in a county that uses the baseline:

| $900,000 home | Down payment | Loan amount | Result in a baseline county |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10% down | $90,000 | $810,000 | Conforming (under $832,750) |
| 5% down | $45,000 | $855,000 | Jumbo ($22,250 over the limit) |
| 7.5% down | $67,250 | $832,750 | Conforming (exactly at the limit) |

The smallest down payment that keeps you conforming is simply the price minus the limit. On a $900,000 home in a baseline county, that's $67,250, about 7.5%. The same $855,000 loan would be high-balance conforming in Monterey County, California, where the 2026 limit is $994,750.

Conventional loans with less than 20% down usually require private mortgage insurance, so run the full payment before choosing a down payment. Our [mortgage payment calculator](/mortgage-payment-calculator/) and the [conventional loan requirements](/conventional-loan-requirements/) guide cover the rest of the qualification picture.

## How conforming limits relate to FHA limits

FHA loan limits are set by HUD but pegged to the conforming limit. For 2026, HUD set the FHA one-unit floor (the limit in lower-cost areas) at $541,287, which is 65% of $832,750, and the ceiling at $1,249,125, the same as the conforming ceiling. The FHA floors for 2, 3 and 4 units are $693,050, $837,700 and $1,041,125. HUD announced these on December 11, 2025. Our [FHA loan requirements](/fha-loan-requirements-and-benefits/) guide explains how FHA differs from a conventional loan.

## Key dates

- **Late November:** FHFA announces the next year's conforming limits, usually alongside its third-quarter House Price Index.
- **After FHFA:** HUD announces FHA limits for the next year.
- **January 1:** the new conforming limits apply to loans Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac acquire in the new year.
