The 2027 conforming loan limit for a one-unit home is **$855,400** in most of the United States, up $22,650 from 2026. In high-cost counties it goes as high as **$1,283,100**. The Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA) announced the figures on November 24, 2026, and they apply to loans Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac acquire during 2027. Anything above your county's limit is a [jumbo loan](/jumbo-loan-requirements/).

The 2026 limits ($832,750 baseline, $1,249,125 ceiling) still apply to loans the two companies acquire in 2026. If you're closing in December, ask your lender which year's limit your loan will fall under.

## Conforming loan limits by property type, 2027 and 2026

| Property type | 2027 baseline | 2027 high-cost ceiling | 2026 baseline | 2026 high-cost ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 unit | $855,400 | $1,283,100 | $832,750 | $1,249,125 |
| 2 units | $1,095,250 | $1,642,875 | $1,066,250 | $1,599,375 |
| 3 units | $1,323,900 | $1,985,850 | $1,288,800 | $1,933,200 |
| 4 units | $1,645,350 | $2,468,025 | $1,601,750 | $2,402,625 |

The ceiling is 150% of the baseline in each column. Alaska, Hawaii, Guam and the U.S. Virgin Islands have their own statutory baseline, 50% above the national one: for 2027 the one-unit baseline there is $1,283,100 and the ceiling is $1,924,650. The highest limit actually set in those areas is $1,299,500, in Maui and Kalawao counties in Hawaii.

## How the limits changed from 2022 to 2027

| Year | Baseline (1 unit) | Change from prior year | High-cost ceiling (1 unit) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | $647,200 | | $970,800 |
| 2023 | $726,200 | +12.2% | $1,089,300 |
| 2024 | $766,550 | +5.6% | $1,149,825 |
| 2025 | $806,500 | +5.2% | $1,209,750 |
| 2026 | $832,750 | +3.3% | $1,249,125 |
| 2027 | $855,400 | +2.7% | $1,283,100 |

Since 2022 the baseline has risen $208,200, or about 32%. Each yearly increase has been smaller than the one before because home price growth has slowed. By law the baseline moves by the same percentage as FHFA's House Price Index between the third quarter of one year and the third quarter of the next. For 2027 that change was 2.72%.

Some lenders started offering early 2027 limits of $845,000 to $850,000 before FHFA announced, according to National Mortgage Professional and The Mortgage Reports. The official figure came in higher, at $855,400.

## High-cost counties: who gets a higher limit

A county gets a limit above the baseline when 115% of its local median home value is higher than the baseline. The limit is set at that 115% figure, capped at the ceiling. FHFA uses median home values estimated by the Federal Housing Administration and applies the highest county median in a metro area to every county in that metro. FHFA also doesn't let a county's limit fall from one year to the next.

Our tabulation of FHFA's 2027 county file shows:

- **127 counties and county-equivalents** have a limit above the baseline (including two in Hawaii above that state's higher baseline).
- **42 are at the $1,283,100 ceiling.** They include Los Angeles, Orange, San Francisco, San Mateo, Santa Clara, Alameda and Contra Costa counties in California, Washington, D.C., most of Northern Virginia and resort counties such as Eagle (Colorado), Teton (Wyoming) and Nantucket (Massachusetts).
- **24 high-cost counties got no increase for 2027.** They include the Seattle area (King, Pierce and Snohomish, at $1,063,750), the Denver metro (at $862,500) and Napa and Sonoma in California. Under FHFA's no-decline policy, their limits stayed at the 2026 level.
- **Other notable 2027 limits:** New York City, Long Island and much of northern New Jersey are at $1,224,750 (up $15,000), the Nashville area is $1,086,750 (up $57,500), San Diego County is $1,143,100 and the Boston area is $977,500.

Search the table for your county. If it isn't listed, your 2027 limit is the baseline.

## Conforming, high-balance or jumbo: where your loan lands

Your loan falls into one of three buckets, depending on its size and your county's limit:

1. **Standard conforming:** the loan is at or below the baseline ($855,400 for one unit in 2027). This applies everywhere.
2. **High-balance conforming:** the loan is above the baseline but at or below your county's higher limit. This only exists in high-cost counties. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac still buy these loans, though pricing can differ from a standard conforming loan. See our guide to [high-balance conforming loans](/high-balance-conforming-loan/).
3. **Jumbo:** the loan is above your county's limit. Lenders keep these loans or sell them to private investors, so each lender sets its own credit, reserve and down payment rules. See [jumbo loan requirements](/jumbo-loan-requirements/).

## The limit applies to the loan, not the price

What matters is the amount you borrow. Here's how the same $900,000 house lands in a county that uses the baseline, under each year's limit:

| $900,000 home | Down payment | Loan amount | 2026 limit ($832,750) | 2027 limit ($855,400) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10% down | $90,000 | $810,000 | Conforming | Conforming |
| 5% down | $45,000 | $855,000 | Jumbo | Conforming |
| 3% down | $27,000 | $873,000 | Jumbo | Jumbo |

The smallest down payment that keeps you conforming is simply the price minus the limit. On a $900,000 home in a baseline county, that's $67,250 under the 2026 limit and $44,600 under the 2027 limit. The same $873,000 loan would be high-balance conforming in Monterey County, California, where the 2027 limit is $1,006,250.

Conventional loans with less than 20% down usually require private mortgage insurance, so run the full payment before choosing a down payment. Our [mortgage payment calculator](/mortgage-payment-calculator/) and the [conventional loan requirements](/conventional-loan-requirements/) guide cover the rest of the qualification picture.

## How conforming limits relate to FHA limits

FHA loan limits are set by HUD but pegged to the conforming limit. For 2026, HUD set the FHA one-unit floor (the limit in lower-cost areas) at $541,287, which is 65% of $832,750, and the ceiling at $1,249,125. HUD hasn't announced 2027 FHA limits yet; it announced the 2026 limits on December 11, 2025. Applying the same 65% and 150% rules to the new conforming limit would put the 2027 FHA floor at about $556,010 and the ceiling at $1,283,100, but wait for HUD's official figures. Our [FHA loan requirements](/fha-loan-requirements-and-benefits/) guide explains how FHA differs from a conventional loan.
